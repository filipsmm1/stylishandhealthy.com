Is Beauty of Joseon Relief Sun Water Resistant? What to Use for Swimming

The brand’s swimming advice, what SPF50+ does not mean, and how to choose a beach sunscreen.

By Filip · Founder and editorial researcher

Published and checked: October 24, 2026

Research-based analysis; products were not personally wear-tested for this article.

Stylish & Healthy6 min readResearch-led guidance
The quick answer

No water-resistance claim is provided for the Beauty of Joseon Relief Sun products reviewed here. Beauty of Joseon tells users of both original Relief Sun and Aqua-Fresh to use a water-resistant sunscreen when swimming or sweating.12 Their SPF50+ PA++++ ratings describe UV protection under test conditions; they do not tell you how long either formula stays protective on wet skin.

Relief Sun is popular because it feels like everyday skincare. That comfort can make it easier to apply generously on a normal day. But a pleasant face sunscreen and a beach sunscreen need not be the same tube. The question to ask before a pool day is not simply “Is it SPF50+?” It is “Does my exact pack claim tested water resistance, and for how long?”

This article checks Beauty of Joseon's original Relief Sun: Rice + Niacinamide (previously called Rice + Probiotics) and Relief Sun Aqua-Fresh: Rice + B5 as listed for the markets cited below. Packaging and regulations vary by country, so use the claim printed on the product you own as the final reference.

What does Beauty of Joseon say about water?

On its original Relief Sun product page, the brand's directions say to apply generously, reapply at least every two hours, and use a water-resistant sunscreen if swimming or sweating.1 Aqua-Fresh carries the same swimming-and-sweating advice.2 That is a clearer answer than trying to infer performance from a reviewer's shower test or from the products' texture.

We found no stated 40- or 80-minute water-resistance duration on these cited listings. That does not mean a single drop of rain instantly erases every trace of sunscreen. It means you should not plan water exposure around an unverified level of resistance. A tube can have excellent daily UV ratings and still be the wrong tool for sustained swimming.

“Rice + Probiotics” and “Rice + Niacinamide” are the older and newer names for original Relief Sun; the brand says the rename did not change its formula. Aqua-Fresh is a separate, lighter product. Our Relief Sun versus Aqua-Fresh comparison covers texture and ingredient differences. Neither difference is evidence that one is a tested sport sunscreen.

Why does SPF50+ not answer the swimming question?

SPF relates to protection against sunburn from UV exposure under the relevant test. PA++++ indicates a high category of UVA protection in the labeling system used by these products. Neither number is a measure of how well a film resists water, friction, sweat or towel drying.

The US FDA explains that a sunscreen sold with a water-resistance claim must state whether it remains effective for 40 or 80 minutes of swimming or sweating under its test procedure. It also says no sunscreen is “waterproof.”3 The exact wording and regulatory framework differ by market, but the buying principle travels well: look for an explicit tested water-resistance claim and duration rather than assuming the SPF number covers it.

The label check

Before the beach, read the tube or outer box for “water resistant” plus a stated time, or the equivalent claim in your market. A vague “long-lasting,” “outdoor,” or “SPF50+” headline is not the same information.

A brief walk in humid air is different from a workout that leaves your face wet. There is no reliable universal sweat threshold at which original Relief Sun “stops working.” Use the situation and product labeling to make a conservative choice. If sunscreen is visibly running into your eyes or being wiped off repeatedly, maintaining coverage matters more than preserving the original layer.

What does 40 or 80 minutes actually mean?

For a product carrying the US water-resistance designation, 40 or 80 minutes refers to tested performance while swimming or sweating. It is not permission to stay in the sun for 40 or 80 minutes without any other protection, and it does not turn sunscreen into a waterproof shield. The FDA and American Academy of Dermatology both advise reapplication, including after swimming, sweating or toweling off.34

Do not use a home “water bead” test as a substitute for a labeled claim. Water rolling off the skin can reflect the formula's surface feel, your skincare underneath, or how recently you applied it. It does not reproduce a standardized UV-protection test after water immersion.

The two-finger amount often shown online is only a rough visual cue because face size and finger size differ. Apply a generous, even layer to exposed skin. The AAD recommends at least about a teaspoon for the face and reapplication every two hours outdoors, sooner after swimming or sweating.4 Don't reduce the amount just because a fluid texture looks shiny at first.

What should a Relief Sun fan do on a beach day?

Keep Relief Sun for mornings when its comfortable finish helps you use it consistently. For a swim or heavy exercise, pack a separate water-resistant broad-spectrum sunscreen with clear instructions for your market. Apply before exposure as its label directs, cover ears and neck, and reapply after swimming, heavy sweating or towel drying. Shade, hats and protective clothing still matter.

If your skin reacts to your outdoor sunscreen, investigate a different water-resistant formula; don't substitute a non-water-resistant daily product for a long water session because its texture feels nicer. A comfortable product only helps if it is appropriate for the exposure. Quick questions

Is Beauty of Joseon Relief Sun waterproof?

No sunscreen is waterproof. The brand advises a water-resistant sunscreen instead of original Relief Sun when swimming or sweating.

Is Aqua-Fresh better for swimming because it feels lighter?

No. Its light texture is not a water-resistance claim, and the brand gives the same advice to use a water-resistant sunscreen for swimming or sweating.

Does SPF50+ mean I can skip reapplication?

No. The brand says to reapply at least every two hours, and water, sweat or toweling can require earlier reapplication.
